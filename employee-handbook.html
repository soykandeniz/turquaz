Welcome

Hoş geldiniz. Welcome to Turquaz — we're glad you're here.

1.1About Turquaz

Turquaz is a Turkish kitchen, bakery and coffee bar in the heart of San Francisco, operated by TURKUAZ SF INC (the "Company," "Turquaz," "we"). We opened in 2025 at 1198 Mission Street with a simple idea: bring the warmth of an Istanbul table — long meals, fresh bread from the oven, strong tea, and genuine hospitality — to a city that appreciates food made with intention.

We are three businesses under one roof, and most of us work across more than one of them:

Side of the HouseWhat We Do
KitchenMezes, kebaps, pides, wraps, chef's specials and full-service dining, plus catering production.
BakerySimit, açma, poğaça, börek, breakfast service, desserts and handcrafted baklava — baked fresh, starting before dawn.
Coffee BarEspresso, Turkish coffee, teas and house specialty drinks served all day.

We serve dine-in guests with reservations and walk-ins, online pickup and delivery orders, and catering for offices and events. Every one of those channels is somebody's real meal, so every one of them gets the same standard.

1.2What We Stand For

  • Misafirperverlik — hospitality first. In Turkish culture a guest is a gift. We greet warmly, we anticipate, and we never make someone feel like a transaction.
  • Clean hands, clean station, clean conscience. Food safety is not a checklist we perform for inspectors. It is how we protect people.
  • Say it early. A mistake told in the first minute is a small problem. A mistake hidden for an hour is a big one. Nobody is disciplined for raising a problem honestly and quickly.
  • Everyone buses, everyone helps. Titles describe responsibility, not status. The dining room is never "someone else's section."
  • Respect is not optional. Not toward guests, not toward each other, not toward the couriers and vendors who come through our door.

1.3How to Use This Handbook

This handbook is a reference. It summarizes our policies, your rights, and what we expect from each other. Read it before your first shift and keep it somewhere you can find it. It replaces all earlier handbooks and any inconsistent policy statements, written or spoken.

This handbook does not cover every situation, and it is not an employment contract or a promise of continued employment. The Company may interpret, change, or discontinue any policy or benefit at any time, with or without notice — except the at-will employment relationship described in Section 2.1, which can only be changed by a written agreement signed by an officer of the Company. When policies change, we will give you the updated pages.

Where a policy tag appears — CA Law SF Law — that policy reflects a legal requirement, and the law controls if this handbook ever falls out of step with it.

Employment Basics

2.1At-Will Employment

Employment with Turquaz is at-will. Both you and the Company are free to end the employment relationship at any time, for any reason or no reason, with or without cause, and with or without advance notice.

Other policies, procedures and benefits may change at the Company's discretion. The at-will relationship, however, can only be changed by an express written agreement signed by an officer of TURKUAZ SF INC. No manager, supervisor, or other representative has authority to promise employment for a fixed term or to alter your at-will status verbally.

2.2Equal Employment Opportunity CA Law

Turquaz is an equal opportunity employer. We make all employment decisions — hiring, scheduling, promotion, pay, discipline, termination — on merit and business need.

Consistent with applicable law, we prohibit discrimination and harassment on the basis of: race (including hair texture and protective hairstyles such as braids, locks and twists), color, religion or religious creed (including religious dress and grooming), sex, pregnancy, childbirth, breastfeeding and related medical conditions, gender, gender identity and expression, transgender status, sexual orientation, national origin, ancestry, immigration status, primary language, age (40+), physical or mental disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital or domestic partner status, military and veteran status, arrest or conviction record, credit history, caregiver or family status, reproductive health decision-making, cannabis use off-duty and away from work, salary history, height or weight, political affiliation, or any other basis protected by federal, state or local law (together, "Protected Characteristics").

This commitment applies to everyone involved in our operations and prohibits unlawful conduct by employees at every level — as well as by guests, vendors, delivery couriers and other third parties.

2.3Fair Chance — Arrest & Conviction History SF Law

Consistent with the California Fair Chance Act and the San Francisco Fair Chance Ordinance, we do not ask about conviction history on job applications and do not consider it until after a conditional offer of employment. If a conviction record ever raises a concern, we conduct an individualized assessment, notify the applicant in writing, and give them a real opportunity to respond with evidence of rehabilitation or inaccuracy before any final decision.

2.4Reasonable Accommodation CA Law

We provide reasonable accommodation for the known physical or mental limitations of qualified applicants and employees with disabilities, for pregnancy and related conditions, and for sincerely held religious beliefs and practices — unless doing so would create an undue hardship.

You do not need to use any special words or submit a form to request an accommodation. Just tell your manager or Abdulaziz Aslan what you need and why. We will engage in a timely, good-faith interactive process with you to identify workable options. Examples in our environment include modified lifting duties, a stool at the coffee bar, additional breaks, schedule adjustments, or an adjusted grooming standard for religious dress.

Confidentiality Medical information you provide is kept in a separate confidential file, apart from your personnel file, and is shared only with people who need to know in order to implement the accommodation.

2.5Lactation Accommodation CA Law

Employees who need to express breast milk may do so during any paid rest break, and we will provide additional break time as needed. Additional lactation break time beyond paid rest breaks is unpaid for non-exempt employees.

We provide a private lactation space that is not a bathroom, is shielded from view and free from intrusion, and includes a surface to place a pump and personal items, a place to sit, and access to electricity. Refrigeration for storing milk and a sink with running water are available in close proximity. To request use of the space, tell the manager on duty — no advance written request is required. Retaliation for requesting or using lactation accommodation is prohibited, and you may file a complaint with the California Labor Commissioner.

A Respectful Workplace

3.1Anti-Harassment Policy CA Law

Turquaz prohibits harassment based on any Protected Characteristic — including harassment based on the perception that someone has a Protected Characteristic, or that they are associated with someone who does.

What harassment looks like

Harassment includes verbal, physical, visual and electronic conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive working environment, or that interferes with work performance. Conduct is harassment where (1) submitting to it is an explicit or implicit condition of employment; (2) an employee's reaction to it is used as a basis for employment decisions; or (3) it has the purpose or effect of unreasonably interfering with work or creating a hostile environment.

Examples include slurs and epithets; "jokes" about a Protected Characteristic; derogatory or degrading comments or gestures; mocking an accent or language; unwelcome touching; and displaying offensive images, cartoons or objects.

Sexual harassment

Sexual harassment includes demands for sexual favors, conditioning any employment benefit on sexual favors, and treating someone adversely for refusing sexual conduct. Conduct that can create a hostile environment includes:

  • Visual — leering; sexual gestures; pointedly looking at someone's body; displaying or sending sexually suggestive images.
  • Verbal — sexual "joking," teasing or comments; sexual epithets; whistling or catcalls; comments about someone's body or appearance.
  • Physical — unwanted touching, brushing past deliberately in tight kitchen or bar spaces, blocking or impeding movement.
  • Advances — offensive flirtation, propositions, repeated unwanted requests for dates.
  • Discussion of one's own or another person's sexual experiences in the workplace.
  • Messaging — obscene or sexually oriented messages by text, DM, staff chat or any electronic device.
  • Retaliatory action against someone who refuses or reports.

This policy applies at the restaurant, at off-site catering events, at Company-sponsored gatherings, in group chats, and on social media. Sexual harassment does not refer to occasional compliments of a socially acceptable nature or to genuinely consensual social relationships (subject to Section 3.6).

Consequence Harassing conduct will not be tolerated and may result in discipline up to and including immediate termination — for any employee at any level, including managers and owners.

3.2How to Report

If you experience or witness harassment or discrimination, report it immediately — you do not have to be the victim to report, and you do not have to be certain. Report to any of: the manager on duty, the General Manager, or Abdulaziz Aslan. You may report verbally or in writing; written is helpful but not required.

A useful report includes what happened, when and where, who was involved, who else saw it, direct quotes if you remember them, and any texts, photos or video.

Supervisors — Mandatory Every supervisor and manager who learns of possible harassment or discrimination must report it to the General Manager or ownership immediately, whether or not the person involved reports to them, and whether or not the employee asks them to keep it quiet. Failing to escalate is itself a policy violation.

3.3Investigations

Once harassment or discrimination is reported, the Company will conduct or direct a fair, timely, thorough and impartial investigation. We investigate every report we receive, even if the person affected asks us not to. Investigations give all parties appropriate due process and reach a reasonable conclusion based on the evidence.

Complaints and investigations are documented, tracked to completion, and kept as confidential as reasonably possible — information is shared only with people who need it, or as required by law. Interview notes, recordings, correspondence and other investigation records are treated as confidential.

While an investigation is open, we may put reasonable interim measures in place, such as a schedule or station change, a transfer of duties, or a paid suspension. These are not discipline. Refusing to cooperate with an investigation may itself result in discipline.

If we conclude that prohibited conduct occurred, we will take prompt and effective corrective action proportionate to the circumstances — which may include counseling, a written warning, retraining, reassignment, suspension, demotion, or termination. We will notify the person who reported that the investigation is closed and that appropriate action was taken.

3.4Anti-Retaliation & Whistleblower Protection CA Law

Retaliation is strictly prohibited. Nobody at Turquaz will be punished, reassigned unfavorably, scheduled down, or treated differently for:

  • Reporting harassment, discrimination or retaliation in good faith, or participating as a witness in an investigation;
  • Reporting a suspected violation of law — including wage and hour, food safety, or health and safety violations — internally or to a government agency;
  • Reporting an unsafe condition, refusing to perform work that would create a real and apparent hazard, or cooperating with Cal/OSHA or the Department of Public Health;
  • Requesting or taking a leave or accommodation described in this handbook;
  • Discussing your own wages or working conditions with coworkers, or declining to disclose your wages;
  • Filing, testifying in, or assisting any agency proceeding.

Reports of retaliation are investigated with the same seriousness as the underlying complaint. Anyone found to have retaliated is subject to discipline up to and including termination.

3.5Harassment by Guests, Vendors & Couriers Turquaz

Our commitment to a harassment-free workplace does not stop at the guest side of the counter. You are never required to tolerate harassment from a guest, a delivery courier, a vendor, or anyone else in order to be hospitable.

  • If a guest makes you uncomfortable, you may step away immediately and ask the manager on duty to take over the table or transaction. No permission needed, no explanation owed to the guest.
  • Tell the MOD what happened. Managers are expected to intervene, and to ask a guest to leave when necessary. Our hospitality standard does not include enduring abuse.
  • Managers will document the incident and, where warranted, refuse future service or escalate to ownership.
  • Tips, sales performance, or a large party's bill are never a reason to leave a coworker in an uncomfortable situation.

3.6Fraternization

We are a small team and we work closely together. Friendships are welcome; relationships that create favoritism or a conflict of authority are not.

  1. Professional conduct on duty. Regardless of relationships outside work, everyone maintains professional interactions on the floor and in the kitchen.
  2. Romantic relationships. Romantic or dating relationships between a supervisor and anyone under their direct or indirect supervision are prohibited. If such a relationship develops, it must be disclosed promptly to the General Manager or ownership so that reporting lines or scheduling can be adjusted. Disclosure is not a disciplinary event; concealment may be.
  3. Friendships. Personal friendships are not restricted, but conduct that disrupts productivity, creates tension, or damages team morale is not acceptable.
  4. No favoritism. Employees in personal relationships must avoid granting or appearing to grant special privileges — preferred sections, schedule preferences, favorable side work, comps or discounts.
  5. Report concerns. Raise concerns about favoritism or conflicts of interest with management. The Company may reassign, adjust schedules, or take disciplinary action to protect the integrity of the workplace.

3.7Drugs & Alcohol

You may not consume, possess, distribute or sell illegal drugs or controlled substances while on duty, and you may not be impaired by any substance — including alcohol and cannabis — while at work, on Company premises, or engaged in Company business. This is a safety rule: we work with open flame, 500°F ovens, hot syrup, vertical broilers and knives.

Prescription and over-the-counter medication taken as directed is permitted. If a medication may affect your alertness, coordination or ability to perform safety-sensitive work, tell your manager privately so we can adjust duties — you do not have to disclose the medication or your condition.

Consistent with California law, we do not discriminate against employees based on off-duty, off-premises cannabis use away from work, or on non-psychoactive cannabis metabolites in a drug test. Impairment on the job is a different matter and is prohibited.

We encourage anyone struggling with substance dependency to seek treatment. Ask about time off for a treatment or rehabilitation program — we will accommodate reasonable requests where it does not create an undue hardship, and we will keep the request confidential. Seeking help voluntarily is not a disciplinary event; a policy violation that has already occurred is handled on its own terms.

Safety, Health & Security

Nothing we serve is worth someone getting hurt. Safety instructions always outrank speed.

4.1Injury & Illness Prevention Program CA Law

Cal/OSHA requires every California employer to maintain a written Injury and Illness Prevention Program (IIPP). Ours is kept in the manager's office and is available to you on request at any time. The General Manager is our designated IIPP administrator.

Your part in it:

  • Follow safe work practices and use the guards, mats, gloves and tools provided.
  • Report every hazard immediately — a wet floor, a frayed cord, a jammed slicer guard, a blocked exit, a broken cooler. You can report to any manager, verbally or in writing, and you may do so anonymously.
  • Attend safety training and shift-huddle safety topics. Training time is paid.
  • Never operate equipment you have not been trained on.
Your Right to Refuse You have the legal right to refuse work that would create a real and apparent hazard to you or others, and to report safety concerns to Cal/OSHA. We will not retaliate against you for doing either.

4.2Workplace Violence Prevention Plan CA Law

Turquaz has zero tolerance for violence and threats of violence. Acts and threats — including those made "as a joke" — are taken seriously and lead to discipline up to and including termination. Possession of a non-work-related weapon on Company premises or at a Company event is itself treated as a threat of violence. Threats may be direct or indirect, verbal or nonverbal.

As required by California Labor Code §6401.9, we maintain a written Workplace Violence Prevention Plan. It is part of our IIPP, available in the manager's office, and covers the four types of workplace violence, our hazard-assessment process, emergency response, and post-incident debriefing. Training is provided at hire and annually, and whenever a new hazard is identified.

Reporting a workplace violence incident Action Required

  1. If there is immediate danger, get yourself and guests to safety and call 911 first. Do not confront.
  2. Notify the manager on duty as soon as it is safe.
  3. The MOD records the incident in the Violent Incident Log within the shift. The log excludes personal identifying information about the person who reported.
  4. Ownership reviews every entry and reassesses controls.

Report all concerning behavior — an escalating guest, a person loitering at the back door, an ex-partner appearing at the restaurant, a threatening message. Early reports are how we prevent incidents. Nobody is penalized for reporting a threat that turns out to be nothing.

4.3Indoor Heat Illness Prevention CA Law

Our kitchen line, bakery ovens and dish area can exceed 82°F. California's indoor heat standard (8 CCR §3396) applies to us, and we maintain written procedures as part of our IIPP.

  • Water. Fresh, pure, suitably cool drinking water is provided free of charge, as close as practicable to your station. Drink often — aim for a cup every 15–20 minutes when working hot stations.
  • Cool-down areas. A designated cool-down area is available at all times. You may take a preventative cool-down rest of at least five minutes whenever you feel the need to protect yourself from overheating. This rest is paid, is not deducted from your rest breaks, and you never need to justify it.
  • Watch each other. Heat illness starts with headache, dizziness, nausea, cramps, unusual irritability or confusion. If a coworker seems off, get a manager. Never leave someone showing symptoms alone.
  • Control measures. When temperatures rise, we increase ventilation, rotate hot-station assignments, adjust prep to cooler hours where possible, and add cooling breaks.
Emergency Confusion, slurred speech, fainting, seizures or hot dry skin are signs of heat stroke — a medical emergency. Call 911 immediately, move the person to a cool area, and begin cooling with water and ice while waiting.

4.5Injury Reporting & Workers' Compensation CA Law

Turquaz carries workers' compensation insurance at no cost to you. It covers medical treatment and partial wage replacement for injuries and illnesses arising out of work — including cuts, burns, strains, slips and repetitive-motion injuries.

  1. Report every injury to the manager on duty immediately, no matter how minor. A small burn reported today is covered; the same burn reported next week is a problem for everyone.
  2. The MOD provides first aid, arranges medical care if needed, and gives you a DWC-1 claim form within one working day.
  3. Complete and return the employee portion of the DWC-1 as soon as you are able.
  4. Serious injuries are reported to Cal/OSHA as required by law.

You may pre-designate your personal physician for work-injury treatment by giving the General Manager written notice before an injury occurs. It is illegal for us to retaliate against you for filing or intending to file a workers' compensation claim, and we will not.

4.6Emergency Procedures Turquaz

Evacuation maps, the fire extinguisher and Ansul pull locations, first aid kits and the AED (if installed) are posted by the kitchen exit and near the host stand. Know them before you need them.

Fire
Pull the hood suppression handle for a hood fire, shut off gas, evacuate guests through the nearest clear exit, call 911. Only fight a small, contained fire if you are trained and have a clear exit behind you. Class K extinguisher for grease — never water.
Earthquake
Drop, cover, hold on. Move away from the hot line, glassware and the bakery racks. Do not run outside during shaking. After shaking stops, shut off gas, check for injuries and gas smell, then evacuate on the MOD's direction to our assembly point.
Medical
Call 911 first. Do not move a seriously injured person. Send someone to the street to flag the ambulance. Report to the MOD and complete an incident report.
Choking guest
Ask "are you choking?" If they cannot speak, act and call for a manager. Call 911.
Power outage
MOD accounts for guests and staff, secures the POS drawer, and logs cooler and freezer temperatures. Do not open refrigeration doors. Follow the food-hold decision tree in Section 5.4.
Robbery
Comply. Do not resist, chase, or record. Money is replaceable. Once safe, lock the door, call 911, preserve the scene and notify the GM.
Active threat
Run · Hide · Fight, in that order. Get guests out through the back if the path is clear. Silence your phone once hidden. Call 911 when it is safe.

4.7Building Security & Cash Safety Turquaz

  • Never prop the back door open. Deliveries are received with a person present at the door.
  • Do not admit anyone after hours who is not a scheduled employee or an expected vendor with the MOD's approval.
  • Nobody closes alone. At least two people leave together, and staff walk out together to the street.
  • Drops go into the safe throughout the shift so the drawer never carries excess cash. Never count cash where it is visible from the street.

The rules

8.6Rest & Recovery Breaks CA Law

Reporting time pay

9.3Pregnancy Disability Leave CA Law

Every employee disabled by pregnancy, childbirth or a related medical condition is entitled to up to four months (17⅓ weeks) of job-protected leave — regardless of length of employment or hours worked.

  • PDL is not automatic for a fixed period; it covers the time your health care provider determines you are actually disabled, up to four months.
  • Covered conditions include severe morning sickness, prenatal and postnatal appointments, doctor-ordered bed rest, gestational diabetes, pregnancy-induced hypertension, preeclampsia, recovery from childbirth, loss or end of pregnancy, and postpartum depression.
  • PDL may be taken continuously, intermittently, or as a reduced schedule; all of it counts against the four-month entitlement.
  • We may require written medical certification substantiating the need, and a release to return to work.
  • PDL is unpaid, but you may be eligible for State Disability Insurance through the EDD, and you may use accrued paid sick leave.
  • Group health coverage continues during PDL at the same level and conditions as if you had continued working, for up to four months in a 12-month period.
  • PDL runs separately from CFRA bonding leave — you may be entitled to PDL and then up to 12 weeks of CFRA bonding leave.
  • We also provide reasonable accommodation and, where medically advisable, transfer to a less strenuous or hazardous position for the duration of the pregnancy.

9.5Bereavement Leave CA Law
